# Specification 006 decision packet

Revised 2026-11-11 after review of published head `b9801de`. All D1–D13 items remain proposals. Approval applies to the named items in [design revision 2](design.md). The [verification matrix](verification-matrix.md) gives the acceptance cases. The [review findings](preparation-review.md) identify the recommendations that changed.

Approving a recommendation approves local implementation with synthetic data and disposable databases only. Push, pull request, merge, native `tracking_sys` migration, real customer intake and deployment remain separately gated.

## Group B: identity policy

| ID | Recommended option | Practical alternative | Consequence of each |
|---|---|---|---|
| D2 Evidence and automatic linking | Automatic links only from **trusted assertions** sent with an ingest key, with an allowed method for that identifier kind and `verified_at` within **30 days**. Keys join only when co-asserted in one assertion. The engine never merges existing persons. This expands a client-granted ingest credential's write authority to include assertions. Acknowledgements disclose no person ID or matching outcome. Browser forms, campaign links, devices and IP addresses never link. No webhook assertions in 006 v1. | Also accept unverified contact values (for example a lead-form email) as automatic links when they match an existing verified key. | Recommended: fewer false merges and a clear evidence chain, but lower recall (Outcome B in design section 2.2). Alternative: more connected journeys, but anyone typing another person's email attaches their visits to that person's timeline, which violates acceptance criterion 2. |
| D4 Phone normalization | `phone:v1` = international input only (`+` or `00`); validate the ASCII international number before canonicalization, domain-separate matching versions and retain applicable versions for deletion checks. National input is rejected with `phone_country_required`. The candidate trunk-zero rule is not used. | (a) Add a per-client default country with the trunk-zero rule (new client column). (b) Add a phone-number parsing library (new dependency) with an explicit per-assertion country. | Recommended: no false merges from country guesses; senders must convert to international form. (a) Merges a Saudi or UAE mobile with an Egyptian landline, mishandles Italian numbers, and false merges cannot be undone without the raw values (design section 5.1, observed on merged code). (b) Correct parsing, but a dependency and metadata update cycle needs its own approval. |
| D5 Conflicts and shared devices | Rules R1-R5 and the segment model of design sections 4.2-4.4: contested keys, an ambiguous outcome with a review queue, `customer_ref` authoritative within its client (one per person), phones as weak anchors that never pull a new email or `customer_ref` into a person (R2-P), a contested key supplies no anchor even alongside new keys, pre-identification events ambiguous on shared devices, cross-boundary late events ambiguous. Persist closed segment bounds across erasure and unmerge. Ambiguous assertions close the prior segment. Call tracker `restart()` on known sign-out or account switch. On a shared site without a reliable switch signal, restrict person-level association to verified account interactions. | Treat any second person on a collection subject as making the whole subject ambiguous (no segments). | Recommended: shared devices keep usable journeys without transferring visits between people. Alternative: simpler code, but a shared family browser loses all attribution after one switch. |
| D13 Lead and customer stages | Design section 4.5: lead requires a **trusted** `lead_signal`; customer requires **verified sale evidence from 007**; browser lead signals and trusted purchase signals are flags only. | Treat any lead_signal as a lead and a trusted purchase_signal as a customer until 007 exists. | Recommended: stage counts are evidence-backed and do not change meaning when 007 arrives. Alternative: earlier "customer" numbers that 007 would later contradict. |

## Group C: consent, handoff and deletion

| ID | Recommended option | Practical alternative | Consequence of each |
|---|---|---|---|
| D3 Identity handoff | One-time handoff code from the tracker (`identityHandoff(challenge)`) bound to the backend's verified form/login transaction, 10-minute lifetime, single use, issued only with effective `identity_link`, redeemable only with the client's ingest key. Direct assertion by bearer token requires recorded server issuance and the current identity grant receipt. | Expose the subject token to the host page so the client's backend can send it directly. | Recommended: one small public route and table, with no browser subject token crossing into the client backend. Alternative: no new route, but a reusable subject credential that can change consent and erase data spreads into client backends and logs. |
| D6 Retrospective linking | R-B: a link covers events of that collection subject received at or after its `identity_link` grant, including events before the assertion. | R-A: all retained events of the collection subject. R-C: only events received after the assertion. | R-B: in the usual one-banner flow the whole session links, and nothing collected before identity consent is linked. R-A links data collected without identity consent. R-C loses the campaign landing that preceded sign-in, which is the main value of cross-device attribution. |
| D7 Withdrawal, expiry and erasure | **W1**: withdrawing or explicitly denying `identity_link` deletes that collection subject's links, assertions and handoffs immediately, and removes persons and keys left without evidence. An ambiguous identity lookup or active merge pauses the affected erasure until original ownership is resolved. A token-holder erasure remains limited to that collection subject. On **expiry**, nothing new is linked and earlier assignments stay until their events expire. **Erasure** follows design section 7 with new tombstone types `identity_key` (over the pseudonym) and `assertion_id`. | **W2**: withdrawal stops new links but keeps past links for history. | W1 matches the 003 propagation rule (linking stops; without another basis, delete) and leaves no linked history the visitor objected to. W2 keeps fuller history for attribution, but continued use of withdrawn linking needs a legal basis the owner must document. |

## Group D: access and corrections

| ID | Recommended option | Practical alternative | Consequence of each |
|---|---|---|---|
| D8 Who may read person-level data | Owner/Admin for all clients; Analyst and ClientViewer only with a new per-client `can_view_identities` grant flag (default off), like the financial flag. No service read-key access in v1. | Any principal with `client:read` sees profiles and timelines. | Recommended: person-level journeys are opt-in per member and client; one schema flag on two grant tables. Alternative: no schema change, but every existing analyst and client viewer immediately sees person-level journeys. |
| D9 Contact values | Store keyed identifiers only. Profiles and the lead inbox show identifier kinds and verification methods, **never values or masks**. Follow-up uses the client's own CRM. The 004 form classes stay unchanged. | Store sealed raw contact values and add a contact-view permission so the lead inbox can show them. | Recommended: no reversible contact store, smallest breach impact, no new key custody. Alternative: a more useful inbox, but new high-sensitivity storage, a display permission, export rules and Q-012 retention, all of which need review. |
| D10 Merge and unmerge | Owner/Admin only, with closed reason codes and expected versions. Disjoint pointer-merge pairs in v1, evidence-anchored associations, reversal of the active edge with ambiguous items sent to review, idempotent operation IDs, and atomic revisions for identity and event changes plus the `identity_changes` feed for 007/008. | Physical merge that rewrites keys and links to the survivor and keeps a history table. | Recommended: unmerge is exact and cheap, and original evidence is never edited. Alternative: simpler reads, but reversal must replay history and loses links created during the merge window. |

## Decisions that actually block the next step

- **006-A (the full 0009 migration)** depends on D1–D12. D2/D3/D4/D5 determine evidence, proof and key representation. D6/D7 determine segment bounds and deletion. D8/D9 determine grant columns and stored contact data. D10 determines merge and change records. D11/D12 determine schema, privileges and retention. A partial selection permits only independent preparation until the affected schema choices have been reviewed.
- **006-B** depends on 006-A. **006-C** also requires D13 for stage semantics.
- **006-D** depends on 006-C. Tracker-only checks in **006-E** depend on 006-B, and its end-to-end timeline check depends on 006-C.

Approving Groups A to D as recommended authorizes all five slices locally. A partial approval should name the groups or items approved, and the slices then stop at the first unapproved dependency.
