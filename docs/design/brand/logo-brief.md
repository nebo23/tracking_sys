# Connect logo brief

Status: closed. Prepared on 2026-11-25 for the owner's review of the first logo. The owner chose option 2, "Pure magnet", on the same day (D-075). The files of that option are in this folder and are described in the [brand README](README.md). The drawings of the other five options were working material and are not stored.

## 1. Why a second round
The first logo drew the C as an open ring with a dot that closed it. In review the owner found that the idea did not carry: the dot read as a ball without a reason, and the lowercase e looked unfinished. The owner asked for a logo that works like a magnet, for research into the best logos and into this idea, for several options side by side, and for a result of international standard.

## 2. What the owner said the brand is
- Connect means money. The product follows everything, from a piece of content to a sale.
- The picture is a magnet: something is pulled, and it should be clear that it is.
- The name as drawn is liked. The e must be an ordinary, complete e.

## 3. The job of the logo
One idea, said once: content pulls customers in, and Connect tracks the pull to the money. The logo has to identify the product in a sidebar at 28 pixels, in a browser tab at 16 pixels, on a sign-in screen, in an exported report and beside an Arabic name.

## 4. Research
How each source was used is stated, as in the [research notes](../../../research_notes/product-design/sources.md).

- Paul Rand, ["Logos, Flags, and Escutcheons"](https://www.paulrand.design/writing/articles/1991-logos-flags-and-escutcheons.html) (1991, read in full). A logo identifies and does not sell or describe. Its effectiveness rests on distinctiveness, visibility, usability, memorability, universality, durability and timelessness. A complex design defeats itself. The one requirement he calls mandatory is that it reproduces in one color and at very small sizes. The widely shared "seven-step Paul Rand test" is a later compilation and is not used here.
- Sagi Haviv of Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv ([Logo Geek interview](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/sagi-haviv/), [99% Invisible](https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/making-mark-visual-identity-tom-geismar/); search-result excerpts): a good mark is appropriate, distinctive enough to be described or drawn from memory, and simple.
- Wordmarks that hide one idea in their letters ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2015/05/01/13-famous-logos-that-require-a-double-take.html), [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/culture/design/11-hidden-messages-company-logos); search-result excerpts): the arrow between the E and the x of FedEx, the arrow of Amazon, the bear in the mountain of Toblerone. In each the word stays fully legible and the idea is discovered, not explained.
- Small sizes ([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/how-to-favicon-in-six-files-that-fit-most-needs), [dev.to](https://dev.to/alphashark/what-makes-a-logo-work-at-16x16-pixels-c4i); search-result excerpts, practitioner advice): judge the 16-pixel version, use one bold shape and two or three colors, avoid gradients and thin lines, and keep the mark recognizable without color.
- The magnet in logos ([DesignCrowd gallery](https://www.designcrowd.com/logo-design-gallery/scientific/physics/magnet-logos), opened in a browser; two web searches): the usual treatment is a literal red or two-colored horseshoe with sparks, in illustration style. No analytics or attribution product with a magnet logo was found; the searches were not exhaustive. [CleverTap](https://clevertap.com/blog/rebranding-clevertap/) reports that it considered a "retention magnet" in its rebrand and did not adopt it (search-result excerpt).
- The magnet as a marketing picture ([HubSpot on inbound marketing](https://www.hubspot.com/inbound-marketing) and agency articles; search-result excerpts): marketers already describe content that attracts customers as a magnet, so the audience understands the picture without help.
- The name ([Stripe Connect](https://docs.stripe.com/connect); trademark listings in search results): many products are called Connect, among them an established payments product. The word alone cannot be distinctive, so the shape has to be. A trademark search remains open.
- The category, from the visual survey in the research notes: blue, indigo and navy dominate, and Inter is the usual typeface. The teal ink and the drawn letters already set Connect apart.

## 5. Criteria
1. The name reads as "Connect" at a glance, in Latin and beside the Arabic name.
2. The idea is one shape, and a person can describe it or draw it from memory.
3. It holds at 16 pixels and in one ink.
4. It carries no currency sign, no national symbol and no trend of the year: no gradient, no shadow, no speed lines.
5. It is calm enough for a product that handles money: no toy magnet in red and blue.
6. It does not read as something else: not a copyright sign (a C in a circle), not degrees Celsius (a C beside a small raised ring), not a colon (two separate squares beside a letter).

## 6. The six options
| No. | Name | What it was | Weighed against the criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The pull | The C is a large magnet that holds the o as a ball between its poles. The lowercase c gives up its two tips, which sit on the magnet as its caps. This was the owner's own direction. | The clearest story. The o is a ball, so the name is read a little less directly, and the detail of the lowercase c is seen only when explained. |
| 2 | Pure magnet | Only the C changes: a magnet with two colored tips. Every other letter is regular. | The most legible and the calmest. One shape, easy to draw from memory, and the mark alone is a strong application icon. The idea is quiet and is found a moment later. |
| 3 | Tracked coin | As option 1, with the o drawn as a ring with a center point: a coin that is also a target. | Says money and tracking. The ring with a point can also be read as an eye. |
| 4 | Lowercase | All lowercase and even in size. The first c is a small magnet and the o is the ball it attracts. | Friendly. The magnet becomes too small to read at small sizes. |
| 5 | Symbol and wordmark | The magnet and ball in a tile that is also the application icon, beside a fully regular name. | Conventional and safe. Two separate parts, so it is less of one piece. |
| 6 | Two poles | No picture. The name is set in two colors that meet where the two n touch. | Timeless in form, but the magnet is no longer seen. |

## 7. Decision
The owner chose option 2, "Pure magnet" (D-075). It meets every criterion in section 5. To avoid reading the two tips as a colon, they sit flush on the arms in the color versions and are separated by a slit only in the one-ink versions.

The pull itself, which the logo only implies, is shown where there is room for it: on the sign-in screen a magnet attracts people, and on the way they turn into sales.
