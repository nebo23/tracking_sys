# Data lifecycle runner
Specification 003-A3/003-A4/003-A5 under D-024, extended for collected data by specification 004 under D-027 and, among later specifications, for retained report exports by 016-C under D-095. Status: implemented and tested on the disposable database. Native tracking_sys rollout needs its own owner approval; it had not been performed when native status was last verified (2026-11-04, revision `0004_identity`).

## What is deleted, and when
`python -m app.lifecycle` runs one bounded pass and prints counts only. Each period counts from the row's end, never from creation alone, and is compared strictly with one cutoff sampled from the database clock.

| Data | Deleted after |
|---|---|
| Browser sessions | 30 days after revocation, absolute expiry, or idle expiry |
| Reset and legacy verification tokens | 7 days after consumption or expiry |
| Numeric verification challenges | 7 days after consumption, invalidation, or expiry |
| Google OAuth transactions | 1 day after consumption or expiry |
| Connector OAuth transactions (009-A, D-048) | 1 day after consumption or expiry |
| Connector webhook delivery records (009-D, D-049) | 7 days after receipt (`TRACKING_WEBHOOK_DELIVERY_RETENTION_DAYS`, at most 7) |
| Connector records (010-A, D-053) | 730 days after observation at the latest (`TRACKING_CONNECTOR_RECORD_RETENTION_DAYS`, at most 730); the disconnect, deauthorize and data deletion purges remove them earlier |
| Rate-limit buckets | At window expiry (an expired bucket restarts anyway) |
| Outbox rows | 30 days after delivery or expiry |
| Password credential, Google identity, sessions, tokens, challenges, and OAuth transactions of a closed account | On the next run after closure, without a period |
| Agreement records of a closed account (017-A, D-092) | On the next run after closure, with its credential state; keeping proof longer awaits legal advice (Q-012) |
| Agency closures whose 30-day grace period has ended (017-A, D8) | Not deleted: the runner marks the agency `closed` and the closure completed (`agency_closures_completed`); see [plans and agency closure](plans-and-closure.md) |
| Development `.eml` and `.pending` files in the mail directory | 7 days after they were written; only in development local mode, non-recursive, never symbolic links |
| Collected event rows, payload and receipt together (004, D-027) | 395 days (13 months) after first receipt; provisional development value |
| Event diagnostic counters (004) | 30 days after their day |
| Rotated-out webhook signing secrets (004) | When their 24-hour overlap ends |
| Collection subjects and their consent records (004) | 395 days after last activity, once no event row references the subject; a 015 booking still linked to such a subject is detached first (both link columns cleared in the same batch, `booking_links`) and kept until its own retention (review finding R002, D-070) |
| Paymob callback diagnostics (015-B, D-067) | 30 days after receipt (`TRACKING_PAYMENT_DIAGNOSTIC_RETENTION_DAYS`, at most 30) |
| Replaced Paymob HMAC secrets (015-B) | When their 24-hour overlap ends |
| Revenue import records (015-C, D-067) | 395 days after the commit (`TRACKING_REVENUE_IMPORT_RETENTION_DAYS`, at most 395); the declared evidence stays |
| Integration deliveries (015-D, D-068) | 30 days after receipt (`TRACKING_INTEGRATION_DELIVERY_RETENTION_DAYS`, at most 30) |
| Replaced integration signing secrets (015-D) | When their 24-hour overlap ends |
| Bookings with their events, and CRM deals (015-D) | 395 days after their last update (`TRACKING_BOOKING_RETENTION_DAYS`, `TRACKING_DEAL_RETENTION_DAYS`, at most 395) |
| WhatsApp referral messages (015-E, D-068) | 395 days after receipt (`TRACKING_WHATSAPP_MESSAGE_RETENTION_DAYS`, at most 395) |
| Staging rows of a failed attribution consumer (008-B, D-079 D12) | On the next run (`attribution_staging`); the consumer state is reset and the next worker pass starts again from the published state |
| Person, lead event and touchpoint pointers of stored attribution (008-B, D9, D12) | Scrubbed in the transaction that erases, expires or deletes the person or its evidence; the runner scrubs a conversion whose owner no longer exists (`attribution_scrubbed`). Shares, amounts and provenance are kept for the workspace lifetime |
| Retained report exports with their sealed content (016-C, D-095 D5) | 7 days after creation (`TRACKING_REPORT_EXPORT_LIFETIME_DAYS` in the API settings, at most 7), content first (`report_exports`, `report_export_contents`, reported only when not zero). A revocation (manual, erasure, disconnection, purge, archival) deletes the content at once and keeps the row until that expiry. A `discarded` export, which never became ready, and a `pending` reservation older than one hour are deleted on the next run |
| Sealed parts of an offboarding export (017-B, D-098 D5) | 7 days after the request (`agency_export_parts`, reported only when not zero); at once at a revocation (a new request or a cancellation); the export row stays with the agency until its purge |
| Every row of a closed agency (017-B, D-098 D7) | Once its offboarding is done and every teardown has settled or passed its 72-hour deadline, through `purge_agency_batch` in batches of `lifecycle_batch_size` (`agency_rows_purged`, `agencies_purged`); the agency row with a pseudonymous name, its plan assignments and its purge record stay; see [plans and agency closure](plans-and-closure.md#offboarding-017-b) |
| `report_ready` messages (016-C, D9) | They expire with their export, so the mail worker clears an undelivered one unsent; the row follows the outbox rule above |

Security events, audit events, invitations, service credentials, and unverified registrations are not deleted, except by the purge of a closed agency (017-B), which deletes the agency's audit events, invitations and service credentials; their retention rules remain pending (Q-012). Revenue evidence (015, D-067 D14) is kept for the life of the client workspace; the runner never reads or deletes it. Erasure tombstones and deletion requests are kept for the workspace lifetime (D-025, D-027) and are never touched by the runner. A closed user's row stays pseudonymized for audit references ([account closure](authentication.md#account-closure)). The approved periods are also the settings' upper bounds: `TRACKING_SESSION_RETENTION_DAYS` and similar variables may shorten a period but cannot extend it. Every new verification, reset, and invitation message carries the expiry of its code or link, and the mail worker clears an undelivered message at that time instead of retrying it. Messages queued without an expiry are outside this cleanup; see [legacy queued mail](#legacy-queued-mail-proposal-awaiting-owner-approval).

## Safety properties
- A dedicated `tracking_lifecycle` role can select and delete rows only in the six credential-state tables, plus password credentials and external identities, and (migration 0007) collected events, diagnostic counters, webhook secrets, consent records and collection subjects. The grants cannot restrict rows; the runner's predicates delete password credentials and identities only for closed accounts, which it finds by reading users. It cannot update or insert anything, cannot change users, tenancy, audit, or security rows, cannot reach erasure tombstones or requests, and has no schema privileges. The one update it may make is the R002 detach of a booking from an expiring subject (D-070): `UPDATE (subject_id, linked_at)` and `SELECT (id, updated_at, client_id, subject_id)` on `bookings`, with no other booking column readable or changeable.
- Collected events, consent records and collection subjects are expired one client at a time. Each batch transaction first takes that client's privacy lock (the lock that event admission, consent changes and erasure take), waiting at most 100 ms, and only then evaluates eligibility. An in-flight admission therefore either commits first, and its subject is no longer eligible, or waits until the batch has committed. A busy client is reported incomplete and finished by the next run; a subject is deleted only after all its consent records (review finding R004-02).
- Committed batches never change effective consent into a grant (review finding R004-04). Admission uses the latest consent record per subject and purpose, by receipt time and then ID. The runner deletes a record only when a newer one of that purpose exists, or when no older one remains, which leaves the purpose unknown, a denial. A withdrawal or denial therefore outlives every older grant it overrides, even when a run stops after any batch. A client's batches continue until one deletes nothing. Erasure, consent and tombstone handling are described in the [event collection guide](event-collection.md).
- Migration 0031 (017-A) lets the role read an agency's ID and status and change only its status and `updated_at`, read a closure's ID, agency, closing time and outcome columns and set only its `completed_at`, and read and delete `user_agreements` by user. The runner moves only a closing agency whose closure is due to `closed`, under that agency's lock; it cannot read agency names, plans or assignments.
- Migration 0032 (016-C) lets the role read only the ID, status, creation and expiry of `report_exports` and the export ID of `report_export_contents`, and delete rows of both; it cannot read a parameter, a revision or a ciphertext, and cannot change or insert anything. Report schedules, their recipients and agency branding are out of its reach. The runtime role never deletes an export row, only an export's content.
- Migration 0029 (008-B) lets the role read the six attribution tables, update only their scrub columns and the consumer state, and delete conversions, runs, allocations and credit changes, which it does only for the staging generation of a failed consumer. It cannot change a share, an amount, a currency, a reporting date or provenance.
- Migration 0033 (017-B) lets the role execute `purge_agency_batch(uuid, integer)` and read only the agency and state of `agency_purges`, the ID and expiry of `agency_exports` and the export ID of `agency_export_parts`, and delete export parts. The function is `SECURITY DEFINER`, owned by the migration role, with `EXECUTE` revoked from `PUBLIC`: it refuses an agency that is not closed, whose offboarding is not complete or that still has a live connection, and one with a teardown before its deadline; it deletes at most its budget of rows per call, children first, and replaces the agency's name with a pseudonym only after a complete walk. The role still has no direct delete right on tenant configuration.
- One run at a time: a second runner finds the advisory lock held and exits with `skipped_concurrent_run`.
- Batches commit separately. A batch waits at most 100 ms for a row lock held by an API request, then rolls back only its own batch; that table is reported as incomplete and finished by the next run. Because every run re-evaluates the predicates, an interrupted or bounded run never skips an eligible row permanently.

## Running it
Docker (disposable or local stack): `.env.docker` holds `TRACKING_LIFECYCLE_PASSWORD`; a fresh database volume creates the role during initialization. Run `docker compose --env-file .env.docker -f infra/docker/compose.yaml --profile lifecycle run --rm lifecycle`. An existing volume created before D-024 has no lifecycle role, so its migration to 0005 stops with a message before granting anything. Recreating the volume is not required: add the role in place as the container's PostgreSQL administrator, with the attributes, password variable, and schema usage that `infra/docker/postgres/init-roles.sh` gives it, then rerun the reviewed migrations. This preserves the volume and its data.

Native tracking_sys (owner-approved rollout step; not performed as of the last verified native status on 2026-11-04):
1. In pgAdmin, run [infra/postgres/lifecycle-role.sql](../../infra/postgres/lifecycle-role.sql) on tracking_sys, then set the role's password.
2. Add `TRACKING_LIFECYCLE_PASSWORD` to the ignored `.env`.
3. Apply the reviewed migrations with the migrator role, check readiness, and only then run `python -m app.lifecycle` from `backend`.

No scheduler is installed. Run the command manually or from an operating-system scheduler; durable scheduling waits for Q-009.

## Legacy queued mail (proposal awaiting owner approval)
Messages queued before expiries existed have `expires_at` NULL: reset and invitation messages queued before D-024, and verification-link messages queued before D-022. While such a message is undelivered, `delivered_at` is also NULL, so it matches neither the worker's expiry check nor the runner's outbox rule (delivery or expiry more than 30 days ago). Time-based cleanup never reaches it. The worker keeps retrying it, because D-024 sets no retry cap, and a message that never succeeds keeps its encrypted payload, with its recipient and link, indefinitely. A delivered legacy message is unaffected: delivery clears its payload, and the row ages out normally. This limitation is not fixed. The number of such rows in native tracking_sys is unknown, because it has not been inspected.

Proposed transition, as one reviewed, owner-run step after the native rollout. It adds no schema, migration, backfill, or retention period, and nothing has been run:
1. Count the candidates read-only, by purpose and creation month: `delivered_at IS NULL AND expires_at IS NULL AND encrypted_payload IS NOT NULL`.
2. Option T1, derive the expiry from a reliably linked token. A one-time reviewed job decrypts each candidate with the mail key, reads the token from its link, and looks up the token's SHA-256 digest in `auth_tokens.token_digest` (reset and legacy verification) or `agency_invitations.token_digest`. Only on an exact match does it set the message's `expires_at` to that row's `expires_at`. The worker then clears an expired payload unsent, and the runner deletes the row 30 days later under D-024. No expiry is inferred for a message without a match.
3. Option T2, retire explicitly. Clear the payload unsent, record a fixed failure code such as `retired_without_expiry`, and set `expires_at` to the retirement time, so the existing 30-day outbox rule removes the row. A link works only while its token row exists and is open, so retiring a message whose token is gone loses nothing; a recipient whose token is still open requests a new link or invitation.

Recommendation: T1 for messages with an exact token match, T2 for the rest. The policy, the job, and their rollout await an owner decision. Until then, legacy rows keep the behavior described above.
