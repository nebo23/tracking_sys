# Market research: client tracking and attribution
Research checked: 2026-10-28. English revision: 2026-10-29; this revision does not refresh pricing or provider claims.

## Scope and method
This is desk research based on official product, pricing, and documentation pages. No paid trials, customer interviews, purchase requests, or demonstrations were conducted. Vendor capabilities are advertised capabilities, not independent proof of accuracy. Prices are historical observations, not quotations or spending authorization.

The confirmed product is a public multi-agency SaaS combining social analytics and attribution from its first release. The owner has not selected an industry vertical. A bounded validation workflow would test the product before broad release; it would not redefine the product as an internal agency tool or remove either capability.

### Interpretation and evidence gaps
A useful combined release needs approved social metrics and a verifiable source/link-to-lead-to-financial-outcome journey. Neither alone meets the confirmed scope.

Fair comparisons require the same numbers of clients, sites, seats, accounts, pageviews/events, payments, history, and connector charges. AI summaries, branded reports, payment links, journeys, and CAPI already exist in competing products. Better workflow execution is a hypothesis to test.

This research does not establish market size, market share, MENA willingness to pay, competitor accuracy, or actual Arabic/RTL quality. Custom quotes and country-specific telephone coverage remain unverified. Older HYROS pages showed USD 230 and different packages, while the canonical pricing page was custom; Cometly descriptive material mentioned sessions while pricing used pageviews. Whatagraph cached snippets described IQ Agents as early access while the live page described inclusion. Revalidate these changing terms before purchasing or specifying dependencies.

## Improvements and claims to test
### Existing mechanisms
VidWorth documents passing video identity through utm_content and a click identifier through tid, then preserving identifiers across forms/schedulers. This demonstrates link-based attribution, not knowledge of every viewer or viewing moment. [Documentation](https://www.vidworth.co/docs)

Dub documents click, lead, sale, and commission flows with click identifiers and server-side capture. Such linking is an established capability. [Official attribution documentation](https://github.com/dubinc/docs/blob/main/docs/concepts/attribution.mdx)

WhatsApp attribution also has existing suppliers. respond.io describes click-to-chat source tracking and conversion events; Wati documents CTWA qualified leads, purchases, CQL, CPP, and ROAS. [respond.io](https://respond.io/faqs/where-does-respondio-fit-in-click-to-chat-ad-setup), [Wati CTWA](https://support.wati.io/en/articles/11463598-how-to-track-ctwa-ad-conversions-in-wati)

For non-Meta advertising, respond.io describes landing pages, click IDs, and prefilled WhatsApp text; text alone does not automatically synchronize Google Ads/GA4 attribution. Wati distinguishes automation-attributed revenue from total store revenue, with deduplication, priority, and historical limits. [Non-Meta workflow](https://respond.io/help/capture-leads/how-to-attribute-whatsapp-conversations-to-non-meta-ads), [Wati commerce analytics](https://support.wati.io/en/articles/15252021-how-to-use-the-shopify-analytics-dashboard-in-wati)

Attributed conversions follow tracking settings and allocation rules. Incremental conversions require an experiment comparing treatment and control. Attribution credit does not prove that an advertisement caused a sale. [Google explanation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14102450?hl=en-GB), [Conversion Lift](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12003020?hl=en)

### Unapproved product hypotheses
1. **Auditable metric evidence:** attach source, last synchronization, model/window, coverage, and unattributed share. Distinguish observed, deterministically linked, self-reported, and modeled evidence rather than inventing an accuracy percentage.
2. **Revenue reconciliation:** separate order-created, paid, settled, refunded, partially refunded, cash-on-delivery delivered/collected, and canceled states. Agree on gross/net definitions; form success or a screenshot is insufficient.
3. **Lead outcomes:** record qualified, booked, attended, won, and lost states with reasons and a CRM or controlled manual source.
4. **Regional workflows:** evaluate Arabic/RTL, EGP/SAR/AED, original currency and dated FX evidence, local payment providers, and WhatsApp operations. These are candidates, not proof competitors lack equivalent support.
5. **Tracking diagnostics:** detect broken pixels/webhooks, duplicates, missing click IDs, expired grants, and delayed payment evidence before generating AI recommendations.
6. **Client service cost:** meter events, storage, provider APIs, and operational effort to inform unit economics and later SaaS pricing.
7. **Reviewable AI later:** ground answers in metrics, ranges, models, and missing-data states. Budget changes require a reviewed action workflow.

Replace promises to track everything with an explicit supported event/source matrix. Video revenue attribution must not become a causal claim about a minute of playback. Referral tokens reveal observed paths, not hidden WhatsApp forwarding. A payment screenshot is a reconciliation input; OCR extracts a claim, not bank confirmation. Describe uniqueness as a hypothesis.

No official InstaPay API was verified here. This market work did not establish detailed Paymob/Kashier capabilities, channel permissions, regional legal requirements, or consent exemptions. Coaching/bookings, commerce/COD, and recurring B2B workflows have different revenue definitions; choose representative acceptance examples without silently restricting the intended market.

## Build, buy, and integrate
The confirmed goal is an independent SaaS. Buying an existing tool is an economic benchmark, not a replacement decision. Cometly's CRM/payment integrations illustrate coexistence rather than rebuilding every source system. AgencyAnalytics and WhatConverts demonstrate that agency/client organization is already expected; VidWorth still depends on recorded provider or manual revenue evidence.

| Approach | Suitable context | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Existing tool | Immediate internal need without a validated differentiator | Same-funnel evaluation, reconciliation, language, permissions, exports, and total cost |
| Custom core with integrations | Distinct agency workflow with mature source providers | API, white-label/resale terms, ownership, exportability, and vendor dependence |
| End-to-end product | Independent SaaS value justifies ongoing connector maintenance | Team, budget, operational capability, platform access, and explicitly deferred scope |

### Proposed validation sequence
1. Define an authorized representative dataset: social account metrics plus content link, owned page, form, booking, and paid invoice. Add commerce/COD/recurring cases according to approved launch scope.
2. Document current metadata loss, lead-stage ownership, payment evidence, reporting decisions, and reporting effort.
3. Conduct owner-authorized interviews: what decision changes, which missing data is unacceptable, who pays, and which channels/languages/currencies matter. No outreach has occurred.
4. Compare an existing tool and an authorized prototype using the same dataset, period, currency, and attribution window. Measure duplicates, missing IDs, financial reconciliation, and isolation.
5. Approve the combined launch scope: selected social capabilities, transparent attribution, a ledger, dashboards, connector health, auditing, and agency/client operations.

Candidates for later review include publishing, broad social listening, a full CRM, replay, probabilistic fingerprinting, MMM, incrementality tooling, autonomous media buying, and every provider simultaneously. These are proposals, not owner-approved exclusions.

No reliable delivery date or budget exists before workload, team, provider, and service-target decisions. No hosting, queue, authentication, ORM, paid integration, or pricing decision is approved. The fixed stack remains FastAPI, PostgreSQL, React, and Docker.
